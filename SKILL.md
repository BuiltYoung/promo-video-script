# Promo Video Script

Made by Built Young (builtyoung.org). This credit is for people reading the skill. Never add it to a student's content.

Turns one product and one idea into a short vertical video a student founder can actually make this week: a script, a list of what to record, prompts for any AI-generated clips, and the caption. Built for Built Young GTM classes, where an instructor runs it and generates the AI clips on the program's account.

## The one rule that shapes everything

**The product on screen is always real.** Screen recordings and phone footage show the product. AI video is only for the scene around it: the problem, the mood, the hook. A generated version of an app or a gadget shows features and screens that don't exist, and a viewer who downloads it and finds something different has been misled. Every script must work with the AI clips removed.

## Workflow

### 1. Intake (ask only for what's missing)

- Product: name, what it does, who it's for, the link
- The one idea for this video. A trend card from the trend-scout skill is ideal; otherwise one problem or one moment
- Channel: TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, RED. All are vertical 9:16
- What the team can record: a screen recording, the physical product, a phone camera, their own voice
- Whether AI clips are available (who holds the account), and how many seconds the instructor will allow
- Founder age bracket, and whether they are willing to appear on camera

### 2. Pick one shape

| Shape | Use when | AI clips |
|---|---|---|
| Problem then fix | The problem is instantly recognisable | Optional, for the problem scene |
| Before and after | The product changes something visible | Optional, for the "before" |
| Demo speedrun | The product is fast or satisfying to watch | None |
| POV / day in the life | The audience shares a routine | Optional, for establishing shots |
| Founder to camera | The founder's story is the hook | None |

One video, one idea. If the team wants to show three features, that is three videos.

### 3. Write the beats

Default length 20 seconds. Structure:

- **Hook, 0 to 3s.** The reason to keep watching, in the first frame. A question, a number, or the problem shown with no setup.
- **Problem, 3 to 7s.** The viewer recognises their own situation.
- **Product in action, 7 to 17s.** Real footage. One task, start to finish.
- **Ask, last 3s.** One action: try it, join the waitlist, follow.

Pacing: on-screen text of 7 words or fewer per beat, and voiceover at about 2.5 words per second (a 20-second video is roughly 45 to 50 words). Write for sound off first, since many people watch muted; the voiceover adds to the text and never replaces it.

### 4. Deliver the script pack

Use this order.

**Script**

| # | Time | What we see | On-screen text | Voice / sound | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Source is one of: `Screen recording`, `Phone footage`, `AI clip`.

**Shot list to record.** Each real shot as an instruction a student can follow: what to open, what to tap, how long to hold, portrait orientation, notifications off, no private information visible on screen.

**AI clip prompts.** One prompt per AI clip, written as below, plus the total AI seconds requested so the instructor can budget.

**Edit notes.** Add all on-screen text and captions in the editor so the wording is exact. Note where cuts fall and which sound plays.

**Caption.** Two hook variants, the caption, and hashtags only where they help.

**No-AI version.** One line per AI clip saying what to film on a phone instead.

**Before posting.** Only the checks that apply (see Honesty and safety).

## Writing Seedance 2.5 prompts

These facts were checked in November 2026. Models change quickly, so when web access is available, confirm against current Dreamina or BytePlus documentation and trust that over this file.

- One generation produces a clip of 4 to 30 seconds, with sound generated together with the picture.
- It accepts reference images, video clips and audio clips. Give each reference one job ("this image is the location", "this clip is the camera movement").
- Timed sections in the prompt control what happens when.

For a promo, ask for short clips: 4 to 8 seconds each, and no more than about 10 seconds of AI footage in a 20 to 30 second video. Short clips cost less, drift less, and keep the product as the main event.

**Prompt format**

```
[Duration]s, vertical 9:16, [look: e.g. natural handheld phone footage, warm daylight].
Scene: [where, what, mood, in one sentence].
0-3s: [one action] · [one camera move].
3-6s: [one action] · [one camera move].
Keep the same: [location, lighting, wardrobe, props].
Sound: [ambience and specific sounds], no music, no dialogue.
Do not include: readable text, logos, brand names, phone or app screens, recognisable real people.
References: [each file and its one job, or "none"].
```

What makes these work: one main action and one camera move per timed block; a specific location, since a vague one is the usual reason a clip drifts between cuts; the same light source throughout; and simple action, because crowded scenes, fast contact and detailed hand movement are where errors appear.

**People in AI clips.** Describe generic, unnamed people, or frame without faces (hands, over the shoulder, from behind). Do not upload photos or video of classmates, teachers, family or anyone real as references. The model restricts real faces, and nobody's likeness should be generated without their agreement.

**Example**

```
5s, vertical 9:16, natural handheld phone footage, evening desk-lamp light.
Scene: a student's desk covered in loose notes and open textbooks the night before a test; tired, overwhelmed mood.
0-3s: slow push-in over the messy desk from above · handheld drift.
3-5s: a hand drops a pen onto the pile and pulls back · camera holds.
Keep the same: the desk, the lamp on the left, the warm light.
Sound: quiet room, paper rustle, pen landing, no music, no dialogue.
Do not include: readable text, logos, brand names, phone or app screens, recognisable real people.
References: none.
```

This clip is the "problem" beat. The next beat cuts to a real screen recording of the product.

## Who can run the AI step

Dreamina's terms set a minimum age of 13 and require a parent's or guardian's consent for anyone under 18, and Seedance 2.5 sits behind a paid plan priced per second. In class, the instructor generates clips on the program's account from the prompts in the pack. A student under 13, or without consent, uses the no-AI version, which is a complete video on its own. Never suggest entering a false age.

Before a clip is used to sell a paid product, the account holder should check their plan's terms on commercial use.

## Honesty and safety

- Never show a generated product, generated app screens, or generated results. Never generate a fake user, a fake review or a fake testimonial.
- Every claim in text or voiceover must be true of the product today. No numbers the team hasn't supplied; use `[NEEDS: ...]`.
- Label AI-generated footage where the platform asks for it, and leave any watermark or content credential in place.
- No real person's face, voice or name without their permission, and a parent's if they are under 18. No voice cloning.
- No characters, logos, footage or music from shows, games or brands. Use the platform's own audio library for music.
- For founders under 18 on camera: first name only, no school name, uniform or home address visible, and a parent has agreed to them appearing.
- Keep it suitable for a young audience: nothing dangerous to copy, nothing that mocks a person or a group.

## Class mode

For several teams at once, give each team one 20-second script with at most one AI clip, and list all the AI prompts together at the end with the total seconds, so the instructor can generate them in one sitting.

## Language

Reply in the user's language. Write on-screen text, voiceover and captions in the language of the channel's audience, natively and not as a translation. Write Seedance prompts in English unless the user asks otherwise.
