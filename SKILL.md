# Promo Video Script

Made by Built Young (builtyoung.org). This credit is for people reading the skill. Never add it to a student's content.

Turns one product and one idea into a short vertical video a founder can actually make this week. It writes the video brief first (storyline, characters, look, sound), then the script, the list of what to record, a full prompt for every AI-generated clip, and the caption. Built for Built Young GTM classes, where an instructor runs it and generates the AI clips on the program's account.

A prompt that only names a scene gives a clip with no story, a different face in every shot and no look of its own. That is why the brief comes before the script, and why every prompt carries the story, the character and the look in full.

## The one rule that shapes everything

**The product on screen is always real.** Screen recordings and phone footage show the product. AI video carries the story around it: the problem, the person, the mood, the turn. A generated version of an app or a gadget shows features and screens that don't exist, and a viewer who downloads it and finds something different has been misled. Every script must work with the AI clips removed.

When the product is a service or a program with no screen (a course, a club, an event), "real" means real footage of it happening, real work made by real customers with their permission, or the real website.

## Workflow

### 1. Intake (ask only for what's missing)

- Product: name, what it does, who it's for, the link
- The one idea for this video. A trend card from the trend-scout skill is ideal; otherwise one problem or one moment
- The viewer: who they are, and what they should feel and do after watching
- Channel: TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, RED. All are vertical 9:16
- What the team can record: a screen recording, the physical product, a phone camera, their own voice
- Who may appear: the founder, generic people, or no people at all
- Look: brand colours, logo file, fonts, and two or three videos or adjectives for the style they like
- Sound: who speaks, in which language, and the mood of any music
- Whether AI clips are available (who holds the account), and how many seconds the instructor will allow
- Founder age bracket, and whether they are willing to appear on camera

If the user pastes a `GTM PIPELINE` or `GTM PROFILE` block, take these from it. The Story, Voice, Look and Material lines of the block answer most of the list.

### 2. Write the video brief first

Before any script, write these seven parts and show them. They are what the script and every prompt are built from.

```
VIDEO BRIEF
Logline: [who] wants [what], but [what is in the way]. [Product] changes [what].
Storyline: hook → tension → turn → proof → ask. One line per beat: what happens, and what the viewer feels.
Characters: one sheet per person on screen (see below). "None" if the video is hands and screens only.
Setting: one or two places · time of day · the light sources in the room · three concrete details.
Visual style: the look (phone documentary, clean studio, screen-led) · palette, with one brand colour as the accent · framing and lens feel · how the camera moves · on-screen text style (font, colour, position).
Sound: who speaks and in what tone · room sound and effects · music mood and where it rises or stops · captions on or off.
Brand: where the logo appears (the end card only, by default) · tagline · the one action for the viewer.
```

**Character sheet.** For each person: role in the story; age range; hair; clothing, in colours; one prop they hold; how they move; how they feel at the start and at the end. A real founder is themselves, described as they will actually appear. A character made by AI is generic and unnamed, and their sheet is written once as a fixed block that is pasted word for word into every prompt.

The storyline needs a change. If the last beat could be swapped with the first and nobody would notice, there is no story yet: go back to the logline.

### 3. Pick one shape

| Shape | Use when | AI clips |
|---|---|---|
| Problem then fix | The problem is instantly recognisable | Optional, for the problem scene |
| Before and after | The product changes something visible | Optional, for the "before" |
| Demo speedrun | The product is fast or satisfying to watch | None |
| POV / day in the life | The audience shares a routine | Optional, for establishing shots |
| Founder to camera | The founder's story is the hook | None |
| Short story | A person's small problem, the turn, and the product as the way out | A story segment, with the product beats real |

One video, one idea. If the team wants to show three features, that is three videos.

### 4. Write the beats

Default length 20 seconds; 15 for one simple point, 30 for a short story. Structure:

- **Hook, 0 to 3s.** The reason to keep watching, in the first frame. A question, a number, or the problem shown with no setup.
- **Tension, 3 to 7s.** The viewer recognises their own situation.
- **Turn and product in action, 7 to 17s.** Real footage. One task, start to finish.
- **Ask, last 3s.** One action: try it, join the waitlist, follow.

In a 30-second story the tension can run to 12 seconds, and the real product still gets at least a third of the running time.

Pacing: on-screen text of 7 words or fewer per beat, and voiceover at about 2.5 words per second in English (a 20-second video is roughly 45 to 50 words) or about 4 characters per second in Chinese (roughly 80 characters for 20 seconds). Have the founder read it aloud against a timer before recording, since people's pace varies. Write for sound off first, since many people watch muted; the voiceover adds to the text and never replaces it.

### 5. Deliver the pack

Use this order.

**Video brief.** The seven parts from step 2, filled in.

**Script**

| # | Time | What we see | On-screen text | Voice / sound | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Source is one of: `Screen recording`, `Phone footage`, `AI clip`.

**Shot list to record.** Each real shot as an instruction a student can follow: what to open, what to tap, how long to hold, portrait orientation, notifications off, no private information visible on screen.

**AI prompts.** One full prompt per AI clip, written as below, each ready to paste by itself. Add the total AI seconds requested so the instructor can budget.

**Edit notes.** Add all on-screen text and captions in the editor so the wording is exact. Note where cuts fall, which sound plays, and where the music comes in. Add the logo only on the end card.

**Caption.** Two hook variants, the caption, and hashtags only where they help.

**No-AI version.** One line per AI clip saying what to film on a phone instead.

**Checks.** The list under "Check the video before calling it done", and the checks that apply from Honesty and safety.

**Finished cut (optional).** If the remotion-best-practices skill is installed and the user wants a finished file, hand it the script's text beats to build the title cards, captions and end card. Real footage and AI clips are dropped in as files; Remotion never draws the product.

## Writing Seedance 2.5 prompts

These points were read in November 2026 from Dreamina's own Seedance 2.5 prompt guide and Luma's guide. Models change quickly, so when web access is available, confirm against current Dreamina or BytePlus documentation and trust that over this file.

- One generation is a single segment of up to 30 seconds, in 9:16, 16:9 or 1:1, with sound generated together with the picture.
- Write the prompt in this order: format, subject, action, place, camera, look, timing, then audio and limits. Put the facts that must not change first.
- Describe what can be seen. "She drops the pen and leans back" works; "she feels overwhelmed" does not.
- For anything longer than one shot, write timed beats. About 6 to 8 seconds per beat, three or four beats in 15 seconds, and the last 3 to 4 seconds for the ending. Too many actions for the time gives rushed, broken motion.
- One shot size and one camera move per beat. Name them: wide, medium, close-up; push-in, orbit, hold, handheld follow.
- Name real light sources ("one desk lamp on the left") instead of words like "cinematic".
- Reference files are attached and tagged in the prompt (`@Image1`, `@Video1`). Give each one job: identity, wardrobe, place, style, camera movement.
- End with a short list of what to keep and what to avoid. There is no separate field for it.
- A good prompt runs about 100 to 180 words. Use the shortest one that fully describes the shot.
- Sound: room tone and effects made by the action come out well. Add music and most speech in the edit.

**Two kinds of AI clip**

| Kind | Length | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| Scene clip | 4 to 10s, one shot | The problem scene or an establishing shot, cut around real footage |
| Story segment | 12 to 20s, two to four timed shots | The opening of a short story: a person, a problem and the turn, before the real product appears |

Agree the total AI seconds with whoever pays before anything is generated.

**Prompt format** (every prompt is complete in itself: the model does not remember the last clip)

```
FORMAT: [seconds] · vertical 9:16 · [one continuous shot / N shots with hard cuts] · [realistic live action / other]
STORY: [one sentence: what happens from the first frame to the last]
CHARACTER: [the fixed character block, word for word] Same face, hair and clothing in every shot.
SETTING: [place, time of day, three concrete details, where the light comes from]
LOOK: [camera feel · light source and direction · palette with one accent colour · depth of field · texture]
TIMELINE:
0-Xs: [shot size]. [One visible action with a start and an end]. [One camera move]. [The sound the action makes].
X-Ys: [shot size]. [action]. [camera]. [sound].
END STATE: [the last frame, held for a second, and what it cuts to]
AUDIO: [room tone and effects]. No music, no dialogue [unless planned].
KEEP: [what must not change: face, clothing, prop, light]
AVOID: readable text, logos, brand names, any phone or app screen, subtitles, watermarks, extra people, extra fingers, cuts or zooms not listed.
REFERENCES: [@Image1 = ..., @Video1 = ..., or none]
```

Set the length and aspect ratio in the tool's own settings as well, and keep the FORMAT line the same as those settings.

**Keeping one video looking like one video**

- Paste the CHARACTER, SETTING and LOOK blocks word for word into every prompt of the same video. Rewording them is the usual reason a face or a room changes between clips.
- Repeat the clothing and the prop inside each beat where the person appears.
- Keep one light source, one palette and one lens feel across all clips.
- For a second clip that continues the first, use the last frame of the first as its start frame.

**People in AI clips.** Describe generic, unnamed people, or frame without faces (hands, over the shoulder, from behind). Do not upload photos or video of classmates, teachers, family or anyone real as references. The model restricts real faces, and nobody's likeness should be generated without their agreement. A founder who wants to be in the video films themselves.

**Example: a 15-second story segment**

```
FORMAT: 15 seconds · vertical 9:16 · three shots with hard cuts · realistic live action.
STORY: The night before a test, a student gives up on a messy pile of notes, then reaches for her phone.
CHARACTER: A 16-year-old student, shoulder-length dark hair tied back, grey hoodie, silver-rimmed glasses, a yellow highlighter in her right hand. Same face, hair and clothing in every shot.
SETTING: A small bedroom desk at 10 pm. Loose handwritten notes, two open textbooks, a mug. One desk lamp on the left; a dark window behind.
LOOK: Natural handheld phone footage. The warm desk lamp is the only light, with soft shadows. Muted colours with one yellow accent. Shallow depth of field.
TIMELINE:
0-5s: Wide shot from above the desk. She shuffles the loose pages, looking for one. Slow push-in. Paper rustle.
5-10s: Medium close-up from the side. She drops the highlighter on the pile and leans back, breathing out. Camera holds still. Highlighter landing, chair creak.
10-15s: Close-up of her hand reaching past the notes for a phone lying face down. Slight handheld drift. She lifts it; the screen stays out of frame.
END STATE: Her hand holding the phone, screen unseen, held for one second. Cuts to a real screen recording.
AUDIO: Quiet room tone and the sounds listed. No music, no dialogue.
KEEP: Her face, hair, hoodie and glasses; the lamp on the left; the warm light.
AVOID: readable text, logos, brand names, any phone or app screen, subtitles, watermarks, extra people, extra fingers, cuts or zooms not listed.
REFERENCES: none.
```

The next beat is the real product on screen.

**When a clip comes out wrong, change one thing**

| What went wrong | Change this |
|---|---|
| The face or clothing changes between shots | Strengthen the CHARACTER block; repeat clothing and prop in each beat; add a reference image for identity |
| Things happen at the wrong moment | Tighten the times in the TIMELINE; cut a beat |
| The camera ignores the direction | One move per beat; remove any second camera instruction |
| Rushed or warped motion | Fewer actions per beat; simpler hand movement |
| Extra people or clutter | Describe an empty background; add them to AVOID |
| Garbled text or a logo appears | Keep text out of the clip; add all text in the editor |

Fix in this order: references and identity, shot structure, camera, then look and sound. Rewriting the whole prompt loses what was already working.

## Check the video before calling it done

```
[ ] The first frame gives a reason to keep watching, with the sound off
[ ] The story has a change: the last beat is different from the first
[ ] The same person looks the same in every clip
[ ] The look is one look: same light, same palette, same text style
[ ] Every product shot is real footage
[ ] On-screen text is exact, readable in one glance, and inside the safe area
[ ] No warped hands, faces, text or logos in any AI clip
[ ] Voice, effects and music don't fight; captions match the voice
[ ] One clear action at the end, and the logo only there
[ ] The length matches the plan
```

If a box can't be ticked, say which one and what to redo. Don't describe a video as finished when only the script exists.

## Who can run the AI step

Dreamina's terms set a minimum age of 13 and require a parent's or guardian's consent for anyone under 18, and Seedance 2.5 sits behind a paid plan priced per second. In class, the instructor generates clips on the program's account from the prompts in the pack. A student under 13, or without consent, uses the no-AI version, which is a complete video on its own. Never suggest entering a false age.

Before a clip is used to sell a paid product, the account holder should check their plan's terms on commercial use.

## Honesty and safety

- Never show a generated product, generated app screens, or generated results. Never generate a fake user, a fake review or a fake testimonial.
- A character made by AI is never presented as a real customer or student.
- Every claim in text or voiceover must be true of the product today. No numbers the team hasn't supplied; use `[NEEDS: ...]`.
- Label AI-generated footage where the platform asks for it, and leave any watermark or content credential in place.
- No real person's face, voice or name without their permission, and a parent's if they are under 18. No voice cloning.
- No characters, logos, footage or music from shows, games or brands. Use the platform's own audio library for music.
- For founders under 18 on camera: first name only, no school name, uniform or home address visible, and a parent has agreed to them appearing.
- Keep it suitable for a young audience: nothing dangerous to copy, nothing that mocks a person or a group.

## Class mode

For several teams at once, give each team a three-line video brief (logline, character, look), one 20-second script and at most one AI clip, and list all the AI prompts together at the end with the total seconds, so the instructor can generate them in one sitting.

## Language

Reply in the user's language. Write on-screen text, voiceover and captions in the language of the channel's audience, natively and not as a translation. Write Seedance prompts in English unless the user asks otherwise.
